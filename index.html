How these digital truth tables work

These tables are arranged differently from the handwritten matrices used in class. In a handwritten matrix, the operators remain in their original positions within the formula, so you must determine the order of the computations yourself. In these digital tables, each calculated column is labeled with the complete subformula it represents, and the calculated columns are already arranged from the first computation to the last.

  1. Read the supplied truth-value assignments on the left.
  2. Complete the first calculated column using the appropriate truth-function.
  3. Move one calculated column to the right at a time. A later column may use a result you already calculated.
  4. The tinted column is the final column. Use it to answer the analysis question beneath the table.

Example: For ~(P & Q) ∨ R, the digital columns ask you to calculate P & Q first, then ~(P & Q), and finally ~(P & Q) ∨ R. The separate computation-order exercises above test whether you can determine that sequence for yourself.
